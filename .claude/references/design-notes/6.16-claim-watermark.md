# §6.16 — design notes (claim watermark)

> Full rationale, history, measurements and rot guards behind the condensed rule §6.16 in `.claude/rules/`. Read on demand when changing this area. Verbatim from the pre-split `performance.md` (2026-11-25).

**§6.16** **A dispatcher's claim starts at a watermark while its claims come back full** (`ClaimWatermark`, one per `WarpDispatcher`, passed to `IWarpSqlQueries.ClaimEnqueuedJobsAsync`; single-worker mode passes `null`). A job that leaves `Enqueued` leaves its claim-index entry behind until vacuum, right where every claim starts reading, so under a backlog each claim walked the dead entries earlier claims had emptied: about 98 index entries read per claim of ten jobs. After a FULL claim from a queue the next one adds `ScheduleTime >= <newest schedule time it took>` (a second cached SQL variant per provider), so the index range scan starts past them; a SHORT claim, and any claim once `HeadClaimInterval` (1 s) has passed since the last head read, reads from the head again. That is what catches a row that became claimable BEHIND the watermark (a scheduled job activated with its original due time, a job a long publishing transaction committed late): it waits at most a second, and only while a backlog exists. No margin before the watermark: 100 ms of margin at 1,000 jobs/s was a hundred dead entries re-read per claim, and the head read covers what a margin would. Measured on the load lab (10,000 jobs, dispatcher, PostgreSQL, after §6.15): claim-index tuples read 93,818 to 22,924, buffer hits 61 to 53 per job. Rot guard: `ConcurrentClaimTests.ClaimEnqueuedJobs_WithAWatermark_SkipsTheEmptiedHeadAndStillReachesAnOlderJob` (both databases; fails without the watermark and without the head read).
