# §6.19 — design notes (dispatcher free slot wait)

> Full rationale, history, measurements and rot guards behind the condensed rule §6.19 in `.claude/rules/`. Read on demand when changing this area. Verbatim from the pre-split `performance.md` (2026-11-25).

**§6.19** **A dispatcher whose group is full waits for a worker to free a slot; it does not poll** (`DispatcherWorkerAvailability.WaitForFreeSlotAsync`, released when the group goes from no free slot to one; a 1 s backstop timeout). It slept 10 ms instead. With quick jobs, the workers empty the group's buffer (workers + prefetch) in about a millisecond, so they then sat idle for the rest of the sleep: one server with 10 workers was capped at ~20 jobs per ~10 ms. On CI (one server x 10 workers, warm, 50k) Warp ran ~1,600 jobs/s with the box half idle and its worker at a third of a core, against ~3,500 for a claim loop that wakes on a freed executor. **Before sizing a claim the dispatcher also settles**: it yields while the free capacity keeps growing (at most 8 yields), so one claim covers every slot a burst of quick jobs frees rather than the first one or two (each claim is a round trip); a handler that holds its slot ends the wait at the first yield that frees nothing. The claim is still capped at the free capacity: nothing is claimed ahead of what the group can hold. Rot guards: `DispatcherWorkerAvailabilityTests.WaitForFreeSlotAsync_*`.
