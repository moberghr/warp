# §2.19 — design notes (lossy pipelines)

> Full rationale, history, measurements and rot guards behind the condensed rule §2.19 in `.claude/rules/`. Read on demand when changing this area. Verbatim from the pre-split `architecture.md` (2026-11-25).

**§2.19** Every "record now, persist/publish later" pipeline uses ONE model in `Warp.Core.Buffering`: **`DrainQueue<T>`** (bounded, single-reader channel; `TryAdd` never waits and returns `false` when full or completed, so the producer counts the drop) + **`DrainService<T>`** (a `BackgroundService` loop: wait, take up to `BatchSize`, `FlushAsync`; at shutdown `CompleteWriting` → stop the loop → drain the rest within `ShutdownDrainBudget`, the budget token passed INTO the flush so a hung database or transport cannot hang shutdown). Users: the adapter, endpoint and client-event recorders/flushers and the #322 `EnqueueNotificationRelay`. A subclass supplies only `Reader`, `BatchSize`, `ShutdownDrainBudget`, `CompleteWriting` and `FlushAsync`, and `FlushAsync` must not throw (an escaping exception ends the loop and, by default, stops the host). `DrainService` is public because `EndpointCallFlusher`/`ClientEventFlusher` are public (registered from `Warp.Http`, §0.5); `DrainQueue` stays internal. The drain contract is tested once (`DrainServiceTests`) rather than per pipeline. NOT for the worker-path `WarpCounterBuffer` (an accumulator summed per key on a timer, §6.2) or `JobLogCollector` (per-job, drained by the job monitor): different shapes, keep them out. A background service costs a few hundred bytes and holds no thread while idle; size the queue's capacity × item size instead.
